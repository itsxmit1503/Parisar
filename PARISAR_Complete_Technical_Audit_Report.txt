  1. EXECUTIVE PRODUCT SUMMARY

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What PARISAR Is
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
**PARISAR** (Hindi: **परिसर** — meaning *campus, premises, or surrounding university environment*) is a role-based university event-management platform custom-engineered for **Dr. Harisingh Gour Vishwavidyalaya (DHSGSU)**, A Central University (established 1946, formerly University of Saugor), located on Patharia Hills, Sagar, Madhya Pradesh (`470003`).


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What Real Problem It Solves at DHSGSU
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Across large multi-department university campuses like DHSGSU (spanning DCSA, Pharmaceutical Sciences, Forensic Science, Law, Commerce, Fine Arts, Physics, and Biotechnology across Patharia Hills), event management traditionally suffers from:
1. **Fragmented Discovery**: Announcements scattered across physical notice boards and unofficial WhatsApp groups.
2. **Unverified Registrations & Seat Hoarding**: Google Forms with no duplicate protection, no real-time seat limits, and no university roll-number binding.
3. **Paper Attendance Bottlenecks**: Paper sign-in sheets at auditorium entrances (like Swarna Jayanti Auditorium or Abhimanch) that enable proxy signatures and delay entry.
4. **Unregulated Event Creation**: Lack of formal administrative oversight from the Office of the Dean of Students' Welfare (DSW) over who can convene official events and book university venues.
5. **Fake Participation Certificates**: Manually distributed PDFs without attendance verification or tamper-evident credential codes.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Core Users (Strict 3-Role Architecture)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Implemented in `src/types/index.ts`:
1. **Student (`student`)**: Discovers published campus events, registers with one click, receives a single-use QR Digital Event Pass (`PARISAR-PASS-*`), tracks participation in the Event Passport, and views/prints verified certificates.
2. **Organizer (`organizer`)**: Faculty conveners, department coordinators, or student society leads. Must pass mandatory DSW verification (`organizerStatus: 'NONE' | 'PENDING' | 'VERIFIED' | 'REJECTED'`) before accessing the Organizer Console to create events, manage rosters, export CSVs, broadcast announcements, scan QR passes, and batch-issue certificates.
3. **University Administrator (`admin`)**: Proctorial/DSW authority who reviews and approves/rejects Organizer Verification Requests, moderates event proposals (`PENDING_REVIEW` -> `PUBLISHED` / `REJECTED`), manages user roles, oversees university-wide attendance ledgers, configures campus venues/categories, and inspects the system audit log.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Why It Is More Than a Static Event Listing Website
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unlike a static bulletin board, PARISAR implements **institutional governance** (two-tier approval: organizer identity verification + event proposal moderation), **stateful capacity enforcement** (`registrationCount` vs `capacity`), **cryptographic QR pass generation** (`qrcode` canvas/data-URL rendering), **5-state turnstile verification** (`SUCCESS`, `DUPLICATE`, `WRONG_EVENT`, `NOT_FOUND`, `INVALID`), and **attendance-gated certificate issuance** (`DHSGSU-2026-*` verification hashes).

PARISAR follows a **Web-Core + Native Android WebView Shell** architecture:
1. **Web Application (`src/` & `web/src/`)**: Built with Next.js `16.3.7` and deployed on Vercel (`https://parisar-eight.vercel.app`). Note that the repository contains a mirrored copy of `src/` inside `web/src/`.

  8. ORGANIZER VERIFICATION AUDIT
====================================================================================================

Verified from `AuthView.tsx`, `OrganizerPendingView.tsx`, `OrganizerRequestsView.tsx`, and `AppContext.tsx`:

1. **Application**: During Organizer Signup, the user submits their Name, Employee/Roll ID, Department, Designation, Email, Phone, and a mandatory **Reason for Organizing**.
2. **Initial State**: Account is created with `role: 'organizer'` and `organizerStatus: 'PENDING'`. An `OrganizerVerificationRequest` record with `status: 'PENDING'` is appended to `organizerRequests`.
3. **Access Gate**: When logged in, `page.tsx` checks `currentUser.organizerStatus !== 'VERIFIED'` and forces `activeTab = 'organizer-pending'`. Even if `createEvent()` in `AppContext.tsx` is invoked programmatically, it checks `currentUser.organizerStatus !== 'VERIFIED'` and returns `{ success: false, error: { code: 'ORGANIZER_NOT_VERIFIED' } }`.
4. **Admin Review**: In the Admin Panel (`OrganizerRequestsView` or `AdminDashboard`), the administrator clicks **Approve Organizer** or **Reject**.
   - **On Approve**: `adminReviewOrganizerRequest(requestId, true, remarks)` updates the request to `APPROVED`, sets the user's `organizerStatus = 'VERIFIED'`, and sends a `POST /api/v1/auth` (`action: 'review-organizer'`) request.
   - **On Reject**: Sets request to `REJECTED` and `organizerStatus = 'REJECTED'` with administrative remarks.
5. **Re-Application Flow**: A rejected organizer sees the red rejection banner in `OrganizerPendingView.tsx` and can click **"Submit New Verification Request"**, updating their designation and justification (`resubmitOrganizerVerification`), which resets their status to `PENDING`.

  9. EVENT WORKFLOW AUDIT
====================================================================================================

Verified from `CreateEventModal.tsx`, `ManageEventsList.tsx`, `EventModeration.tsx`, and `AppContext.tsx`:

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> DRAFT: Organizer clicks "Save as Draft"
    [*] --> PENDING_REVIEW: Organizer clicks "Submit for Review"
```

- **Venue Conflict Detection**: `checkVenueConflict(venueId, startTime, endTime)` checks if any non-cancelled, non-rejected event in the same `venueId` has overlapping timestamps (`s1 < e2 && s2 < e1`). `CreateEventModal.tsx` displays a live warning banner if a conflict is detected, and `createEvent()` blocks submission (`VENUE_CONFLICT`) unless saving as `DRAFT`.
- **Visibility Rules**: Students only see events where `status === 'PUBLISHED' || status === 'APPROVED' || status === 'COMPLETED'`. `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `REJECTED`, and `CANCELLED` events are hidden from the Student Home and Event Discovery grids.
- **Seat Capacity Enforcement**: Every confirmed registration increments `event.registrationCount` by `1`; cancelling decrements it by `1`. When `registrationCount >= capacity`, `registerForEvent()` returns `CAPACITY_FULL` (`"Seat capacity reached. No remaining passes available."`).

  13. QR / ATTENDANCE AUDIT
====================================================================================================


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  How QR Pass Generation Works
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **Token Creation**: When a student registers in `AppContext.tsx`, a token is created:
   ```ts
   const prefix = event.title.split(' ').slice(0, 2).map(w => w[0]).join('').toUpperCase();
   const rollSuffix = (currentUser.rollNumber || '001').slice(-4);
   const qrToken = `PARISAR-PASS-${prefix}-${rollSuffix}-${Math.floor(100 + Math.random() * 900)}`;
   ```
2. **Visual QR Rendering**: `EventPassModal.tsx` passes `registration.qrToken` into `QRCode.toDataURL()` from the `qrcode` library, rendering a 220x220px `#18212B` on `#FCFAF5` QR image inside a perforated ticket card.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  How Turnstile Verification Works (`verifyAndCheckIn` in [`AppContext.tsx`](file:///d:/Worksss/Campus%20Event%20Management/src/context/AppContext.tsx#L1087-L1155))
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When an organizer scans or enters a token in `QRScannerView.tsx`, `verifyAndCheckIn(eventId, qrToken, method)` executes a 4-stage validation:
1. **Format Check**: Must start with `'PARISAR-'` and have length >= 10; otherwise returns `status: 'INVALID'`.
2. **Lookup**: Searches `registrations` for `r.qrToken.toUpperCase() === cleanToken && r.status === 'CONFIRMED'`. If not found, returns `status: 'INVALID'` (displayed as `REGISTRATION NOT FOUND` when triggered via the `NOT_FOUND` test button).
3. **Cross-Event Check**: If `matchingReg.eventId !== eventId`, returns `status: 'WRONG_EVENT'` along with the actual event title the pass belongs to.
4. **Duplicate Check-In Guard**: Checks if `matchingReg.checkedInAt` is truthy or if an `AttendanceRecord` already exists for `(eventId, registrationId)`. If so, returns `status: 'DUPLICATE'` with the exact timestamp of first entry.
5. **Success Commit**: Sets `checkedInAt` on the registration and inserts an `AttendanceRecord` (`method: 'qr' | 'manual'`).

  23. TECHNOLOGY DECISION ANALYSIS
====================================================================================================


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Next.js 16.3.7 (App Router + Client State Router)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`PROBLEM` -> Need a unified codebase that supports clean URLs (`/student/events`, `/organizer/scanner`, `/admin/organizer-requests`), instant tab transitions without page reloads, and built-in serverless API endpoints on Vercel.
`DECISION` -> Used Next.js `16.3.7` with App Router directories delegating to `<ParisarRouteApp>` (`src/app/page.tsx`) and `/api/v1/auth` (`src/app/api/v1/auth/route.ts`).
`ALTERNATIVE` -> Separate Vite SPA + external Express hosting.
`TRADE-OFF` -> Deploying a single Next.js repo on Vercel gives zero-config API + UI hosting and instant client-side state transitions, at the cost of bundling all role views into a single client-side hydration tree.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  2. Hybrid `localStorage` + `/api/v1/auth` In-Memory Store
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`PROBLEM` -> Judges and users testing the live URL need pre-populated, realistic DHSGSU data that responds instantaneously (<50ms) and works resiliently even if an external database isn't provisioned.
`DECISION` -> Built `AppContext.tsx` with `localStorage` (`parisar_dhsgsu_v2_*`) hydrated from `mockData.ts` and synchronized with `serverStore.ts` via `/api/v1/auth`.
`ALTERNATIVE` -> Strict cloud MongoDB Atlas dependency for every read/write.
`TRADE-OFF` -> Guarantees 100% uptime and zero database latency during hackathon demos, but serverless cold starts on Vercel reset `globalThis.__PARISAR_SHARED_DB__`, meaning newly created accounts are ephemeral across server restarts until a persistent DB (e.g., MongoDB Atlas / Postgres) is attached.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  3. Native Java Android WebView Wrapper (`android/`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`PROBLEM` -> Need a downloadable, installable `.apk` (`4.6 MB`) for Android phones that stays 100% in sync with every UI and authentication update deployed to the web app.
`DECISION` -> Built a native Java Android app (`MainActivity.java`) using `android.webkit.WebView` (`OVER_SCROLL_NEVER`, custom offline screen, hardware back navigation) compiled via GitHub Actions.
`ALTERNATIVE` -> Separate React Native or Flutter codebase.
`TRADE-OFF` -> Eliminates UI drift between Web and Android and produces a tiny ~4.6 MB APK with automated CI/CD releases, at the trade-off that it requires an internet connection to load the web shell and lacks native camera hardware API bridging.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  4. Tailwind CSS v4 + Custom "Tactile Academic Ledger" Tokens
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`PROBLEM` -> Generic SaaS dark-mode/neon templates look inappropriate for a historic 1946 Central University (`DHSGSU`).
`DECISION` -> Defined a bespoke warm parchment/sandstone (`#F4F0E8`), terracotta (`#B6533C`), and deep ink (`#18212B`) token system in `globals.css` with hard 2px offset shadows (`shadow-[2px_2px_0_0_#18212B]`).
`ALTERNATIVE` -> Pre-built component library like Material UI (MUI) or Ant Design.
`TRADE-OFF` -> Creates an unmistakable, high-contrast, sunlight-readable visual identity tailored to Sagar's sandstone architecture, requiring custom UI primitives (`Button.tsx`, `Modal.tsx`, `Badge.tsx`).

  [ Q1: What does PARISAR mean, and why did you build this specifically for DHSGSU? ]
- **Short Answer (15–20s)**: *Parisar* (परिसर) means "university campus." We built PARISAR for Dr. Harisingh Gour Vishwavidyalaya (DHSGSU), Sagar, to replace fragmented WhatsApp notices, Google Forms, and paper attendance sheets with a single role-governed platform for students, verified organizers, and the Dean of Students' Welfare.

PARISAR unifies this into an institutional ledger with three strict roles: Students discover and get QR passes; Organizers must be verified by the university before creating events or scanning attendance; and Administrators moderate both organizers and event proposals.
- **Likely Follow-Up**: Why only three roles instead of adding Faculty, Club President, or Volunteer roles?
- **Strong Technical Point**: Keeping RBAC strictly to `student`, `organizer` (with stateful `organizerStatus`), and `admin` avoids role explosion while modeling faculty vs. student conveners through the `designation` and `organizerStatus` attributes.
- **Trap to Avoid**: Never claim PARISAR is a generic multi-college SaaS; emphasize its deep institutional binding to DHSGSU venues, roll numbers, and governance.


  [ Q2: How does PARISAR fulfill the three core challenge requirements? ]
- **Short Answer**: (1) It displays categorized seminars, workshops, cultural events, and competitions on the Student Home and Discovery screens; (2) Students register with one click to receive a QR pass; (3) Organizers manage participant rosters, scan QR passes for attendance, export CSVs, and issue certificates.
- **Detailed Answer**: First, `EventDiscovery.tsx` and `StudentHome.tsx` showcase upcoming events with real-time seat counters, category filters, and an interactive Patharia Hills venue map. Second, `EventDetailModal.tsx` executes atomic seat-capacity, deadline, and duplicate checks via `registerForEvent()` and generates a `PARISAR-PASS-*` QR code using `qrcode`. Third, `OrganizerDashboard.tsx`, `ParticipantsView.tsx`, and `QRScannerView.tsx` allow verified organizers to validate passes across 5 turnstile states, perform manual overrides, export CSV rosters, broadcast announcements, and issue attendance-gated certificates.
- **Likely Follow-Up**: Can a student get a certificate just by registering?
- **Strong Technical Point**: No—`issueCertificatesForEvent()` in `AppContext.tsx` queries the `attendance` ledger, not `registrations`, making proxy certificates impossible.
- **Trap to Avoid**: Do not say certificates are emailed as PDFs; state clearly they are issued in-app with verification hashes and printed via `window.print()`.

  [ Q3: Walk us through your actual tech stack and how the Web and Android apps relate. ]

The Android app is a native Java Android SDK (`compileSdk 34`) WebView wrapper that loads the live Vercel application with native offline handling and disabled overscroll.

- **Short Answer**: `/api/v1/auth/route.ts` is our active Next.js serverless backend deployed on Vercel so the frontend and API run on the same origin without external hosting dependencies. `server/index.js` is our standalone Express + JWT + Mongoose service prepared for dedicated backend deployment.
- **Detailed Answer**: During deployment on Vercel, hosting the authentication and state-sync API inside Next.js App Router (`/api/v1/auth`) allowed both the web browser and the Android WebView to hit `https://parisar-eight.vercel.app/api/v1/auth` with zero CORS issues or cold-start spin-up delays from free-tier external containers. Meanwhile, `server/index.js` contains the full standalone Express REST architecture with `jsonwebtoken` middleware and optional `mongoose` connection for migrating to a dedicated Node.js server.
- **Likely Follow-Up**: Which backend does the live app currently call?
- **Strong Technical Point**: `AppContext.tsx` calls `/api/v1/auth` (the Next.js App Router endpoint) for login, student/organizer signup, organizer review, and state synchronization, while managing event/registration mutations in `AppContext` + `localStorage`.
- **Trap to Avoid**: Don't claim every button click hits MongoDB on Render right now; explain the hybrid `localStorage` + `/api/v1/auth` serverless store honestly.

  Category 3: Authentication, Security & Database Questions
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


  [ Q5: How does authentication work across Web and Android? What happens when a new user signs up? ]
- **Short Answer**: When a user signs up on Web or Android, `AuthView.tsx` calls `POST /api/v1/auth`, which validates uniqueness of their Email and Roll Number, appends the account to the shared server store (`serverStore.ts`), and persists the session locally in `localStorage`.
- **Detailed Answer**: PARISAR supports multi-identifier login—users can sign in with either their university email (`amit.sharma@dhsgsu.edu.in`) or their DHSGSU Roll/Employee ID (`Y23141042` or `EMP-DCSA-104`). On login or signup, the client sends a `POST` request to `/api/v1/auth`. Furthermore, when `AppContext` initializes, it runs `syncWithBackend()`, pulling the latest server snapshot (`GET /api/v1/auth`) and syncing any local accounts (`action: 'sync-state'`).
- **Likely Follow-Up**: Why did a newly created web account sometimes say "Account not found" on the phone if the server restarted?
- **Strong Technical Point**: Because `/api/v1/auth` on Vercel currently stores state in `globalThis.__PARISAR_SHARED_DB__` (in-memory serverless singleton) rather than an external cloud database. When a Vercel serverless function cold-starts, its memory resets to `INITIAL_USERS`. If the web browser is open, its `sync-state` call re-hydrates the server memory; for permanent cross-device persistence across cold starts, connecting `/api/v1/auth` to MongoDB Atlas or Vercel KV/Postgres is the exact production upgrade path.
- **Trap to Avoid**: Never pretend `globalThis` in a serverless function is a permanent disk database—explaining serverless cold-start ephemerality shows strong cloud engineering maturity.


  [ Q6: How do you prevent an unverified student from pretending to be an Organizer and creating fake campus events? ]
- **Short Answer**: Through a mandatory two-stage administrative gate: first, Organizer accounts start with `organizerStatus: 'PENDING'` and are locked to the Verification Pending screen until approved by the Admin; second, even verified organizers' events enter `PENDING_REVIEW` and require Admin approval before becoming visible to students.
- **Detailed Answer**: In `src/app/page.tsx`, `getEffectiveTab()` inspects `currentUser.role === 'organizer'` and `currentUser.organizerStatus !== 'VERIFIED'`. Any unverified or rejected organizer is strictly routed to `OrganizerPendingView.tsx` and cannot render `OrganizerDashboard` or `CreateEventModal`. At the data layer in `AppContext.tsx`, `createEvent()` also verifies `currentUser.organizerStatus === 'VERIFIED'`. Once verified by the Admin (`OrganizerRequestsView.tsx`), any event they submit defaults to `status: 'PENDING_REVIEW'`, which is filtered out of student views (`EventDiscovery.tsx`) until an Admin approves it to `PUBLISHED` in `EventModeration.tsx`.
- **Likely Follow-Up**: What if an organizer's verification request is rejected? Are they permanently locked out?
- **Strong Technical Point**: No—`OrganizerPendingView.tsx` detects `organizerStatus === 'REJECTED'`, displays the Admin's specific `reviewRemarks`, and provides a re-application form (`resubmitOrganizerVerification()`) so they can submit updated faculty/HOD endorsement details.
- **Trap to Avoid**: Don't say organizers can immediately publish events upon signing up.


  [ Q7: What are the current security limitations in your prototype, and how would you harden them for production at DHSGSU? ]
- **Short Answer**: Currently, passwords in seed/local state are plaintext, Next.js auth tokens are base64url-encoded without HMAC signing, and role state is cached in `localStorage`. For production, we would hash passwords with `bcrypt`, enforce `HS256` JWT cookies (`HttpOnly`), and validate all role mutations server-side against MongoDB/PostgreSQL.
- **Detailed Answer**: We prioritized zero-latency demonstrability for the prototype, which introduced three deliberate trade-offs we have audited:
  1. **Password Storage**: Replace plaintext comparisons in `/api/v1/auth` with `bcrypt.hash(password, 12)` and `bcrypt.compare()`.
  2. **Token Signing & Transport**: Replace `createAuthToken()`'s unsigned base64url string with `jsonwebtoken` (`HS256`, already implemented in `server/index.js`) stored in `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict` cookies instead of `localStorage`.
  3. **Endpoint Authorization**: Protect `review-organizer` and `sync-state` on `/api/v1/auth` with server-side admin middleware so client requests cannot self-approve roles.

  Category 4: QR Scanner, Conflict Detection & UX Questions
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


  [ Q8: How does your QR Attendance Scanner work, and what edge cases does it catch? ]
- **Short Answer**: Each registration generates a unique `PARISAR-PASS-*` token encoded into a QR image via `qrcode`. In `QRScannerView.tsx`, `verifyAndCheckIn()` validates the token against 5 states: `SUCCESS`, `DUPLICATE` (already scanned), `WRONG_EVENT` (pass belongs to another event), `NOT_FOUND`, and `INVALID`.
- **Detailed Answer**: When an organizer selects their active session in `QRScannerView.tsx` and scans or inputs a token, `verifyAndCheckIn(selectedEventId, token, method)` runs in `AppContext.tsx`. It first checks the `PARISAR-` prefix, then queries `registrations` for a `CONFIRMED` match. If the pass is for a different event (e.g., scanning an *Abhivyakti Youth Fest* pass at the *Deep Learning Workshop*), it returns `WRONG_EVENT` and names the other event. If `checkedInAt` is already populated, it blocks re-entry with `DUPLICATE` and shows the exact timestamp of the first scan. For the demo, we included 4 one-click test pass triggers and manual token entry alongside the optical reticle UI.
- **Likely Follow-Up**: Does the scanner use the device camera right now?
- **Strong Technical Point**: In the current build, the optical viewfinder is a simulated UI bezel paired with manual token entry and 4 live test-case triggers so judges can test all 5 verification outcomes on any desktop or phone without needing a second physical device displaying a QR code. Adding live camera decoding requires wiring `html5-qrcode` or the Web `BarcodeDetector` API into `QRScannerView.tsx`'s `handleVerify` callback.
- **Trap to Avoid**: Never claim the animated reticle is actively reading from the webcam if a judge holds a phone up to the laptop screen—proactively point out the 4 Turnstile Verification Test buttons!


  [ Q9: How do you prevent two organizers from booking Swarna Jayanti Auditorium at the exact same time? ]
- **Short Answer**: `CreateEventModal.tsx` runs `checkVenueConflict(venueId, startTime, endTime)` in real time, checking if any active event at that venue has overlapping start and end timestamps (`s1 < e2 && s2 < e1`), and blocks submission if a conflict exists.
- **Detailed Answer**: Every event is bound to a specific `venueId` from `CAMPUS_VENUES` and has ISO `startTime` and `endTime` strings. As the organizer selects a venue, date, and time window in `CreateEventModal.tsx`, `useMemo` invokes `checkVenueConflict()`. If another non-cancelled, non-rejected event occupies that `venueId` during an overlapping interval, a warning banner alerts the organizer with the conflicting event's title, and `createEvent()` refuses to submit the proposal for review.


  [ Q10: Why did you disable pull-to-refresh on mobile, and how did you design the UI for first-time students? ]
- **Short Answer**: Browser-style pull-to-refresh makes a WebView app feel like a fragile website and causes accidental reloads while scrolling lists. We disabled it in both CSS (`overscroll-behavior: none`) and Android (`OVER_SCROLL_NEVER`), and simplified every screen around "One Screen = One Clear Purpose."
- **Detailed Answer**: On Android (`MainActivity.java`), we set `webView.setOverScrollMode(View.OVER_SCROLL_NEVER)` and in `globals.css` we applied `overscroll-behavior-y: none`, ensuring scrolling feels like a native app. For UX, we eliminated multi-button clutter on event cards—cards on `StudentHome.tsx` and `EventDiscovery.tsx` show only the image, category, title, date/time, venue, status badge, and a single `View Event →` action, moving the registration action cleanly inside `EventDetailModal.tsx`.
